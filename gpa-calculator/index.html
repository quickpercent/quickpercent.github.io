How to Calculate Semester GPA with Credit Hours

The Standard 4.0 GPA Scale Chart

Most high schools and colleges in the US and internationally use the unweighted 4.0 GPA scale to evaluate academic performance:

  • A (4.0 Points): 90% – 100% (Excellent)
  • A- (3.7 Points): 87% – 89%
  • B+ (3.3 Points): 83% – 86%
  • B (3.0 Points): 80% – 82% (Above Average)
  • B- (2.7 Points): 77% – 79%
  • C+ (2.3 Points): 73% – 76%
  • C (2.0 Points): 70% – 72% (Average)
  • D (1.0 Point): 60% – 69% (Below Average)
  • F (0.0 Points): Below 60% (Failing Grade)

Formula Used by Our Semester GPA Calculator

The calculation uses the standard formula: GPA = Total Grade Points ÷ Total Attempted Credit Hours.

For example, if you earn an 'A' (4.0) in a 3-credit class, your grade points for that class equal 4.0 × 3 = 12 total grade points.

GPA Calculator Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is the difference between an unweighted and weighted GPA calculator?

An unweighted GPA calculator measures grades on a strict 0.0 to 4.0 scale regardless of class difficulty. A weighted GPA calculator factors in AP, IB, or Honors classes, scaling up to 5.0 for advanced subjects.

2. How do credit hours affect my college GPA calculation?

Courses with higher credit hours carry more weight. For instance, getting an 'A' in a 4-credit science lab impacts your total GPA significantly more than getting an 'A' in a 1-credit seminar.

3. What is considered a good college GPA?

A GPA of 3.0 (B average) or higher is generally considered good in college. A GPA of 3.5 or above is often required for Dean's List recognition, honors, and competitive graduate school admissions.

4. What is the difference between GPA and CGPA?

GPA measures academic performance for a single semester or term, whereas CGPA (Cumulative GPA) measures your overall average across all completed semesters combined.

5. Can I use this tool as a high school GPA calculator?

Yes, this tool works perfectly for high school students using the standard 4.0 scale to calculate term grades and GPA averages.
